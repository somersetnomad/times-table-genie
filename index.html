      For grown-ups

      Why rebuilding beats rote

      Memory gets a back-up

      A child who only memorised 6 × 9 is stuck when it slips. A child who knows 5 × 9 + 9 can always get there, and the fact sticks faster because it means something.

      Four tables do the heavy lifting

      With ×1, ×2, ×5 and ×10 secure, every other fact up to 12 × 12 can be built by doubling, halving, adding or taking away a group.

      Try it with real objects

      Lay out six rows of nine buttons or pasta. Cover one row, count the five rows, then add the hidden row back. The splitter does the same thing on screen.
